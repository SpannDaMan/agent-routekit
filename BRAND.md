## Brand thesis

Agent RouteKit makes agent and model routing inspectable. It chooses the least-cost route that still clears the declared quality, complexity, risk, capability, and independence requirements, then leaves a receipt that explains the decision.

The product should feel like a precise piece of routing infrastructure: compact, legible, trustworthy, and easy to inspect. It should not look like a generic AI assistant, a crypto token, or a sprawling enterprise platform.

## Audience

The first user is a Codex power user or agent-framework developer who is already juggling multiple model lanes and wants a policy they can understand, test, and review. The first useful moment is a successful route plan and readable receipt in under five minutes.

## Product language

Use:

- route
- quality floor
- risk
- capability
- independent verifier
- routing receipt
- execution evidence
- fail closed

Avoid:

- magical orchestration
- autonomous intelligence
- infinitely scalable
- self-improving brain
- guaranteed cheapest
- proof of runtime execution

The canonical public promise is: “Route each Codex task to the least expensive eligible model lane, then show exactly why that lane won.”

## Voice

Direct, technical, calm, and verifiable. Prefer a short concrete explanation over hype. State limitations next to claims rather than hiding them in footnotes.

## Proof object

The routing receipt is the defining proof object. Visuals and copy should reinforce a route entering a quality gate and exiting with a verified decision.

## Do

- Use one strong routing symbol that survives at 16–32 px.
- Use restrained developer-tool colors and high contrast.
- Show deterministic receipts and rejected alternatives in demos.
- Keep the first release local, credential-free, and provider-neutral.

## Do not

- Use robot heads, brains, sparkles, chat bubbles, circuit-board clutter, or orbital AI motifs.
- Put tiny text inside the icon.
- imply that a planning receipt proves which model actually ran.
- turn the first release into a hosted proxy, dashboard, or enterprise control plane.
